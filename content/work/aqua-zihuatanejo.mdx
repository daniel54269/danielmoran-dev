## The request

An owner of three beachfront bungalows in Zihuatanejo wanted "a simple landing page and 3 url links."
He had the property on Airbnb and no website. Asked what the business was, who the page was for, and
whether he had a logo, he replied: "All yes."

That answer is the actual constraint. A busy owner will not write you a creative brief. The job is to
need as little from him as possible.

## What I did instead of asking again

He sent three Airbnb links. Everything the page needed was already inside them.

- **Fetched each listing** with a mobile user-agent. Airbnb server-renders a JSON blob into the HTML,
  so the title, description, capacity, rating, review count, and every photo URL come back from a
  plain request. No browser automation, no login, no scraping behind a wall.
- **Pulled the real copy.** "Oasis Bungalow is the largest of 3 completely private units at Aqua" is
  his sentence, not mine. Owners recognise their own words, which makes approval faster.
- **Downloaded six photos per unit** and reviewed them as a contact sheet rather than guessing from
  filenames. The hero I picked is the aerial where AQUA is painted on the building.
- **Built one static page**: a hero, three unit cards with honest ratings, an amenity row, and a
  swipeable gallery. Mobile first, since it would be opened in a text thread.
- **Deployed to GitHub Pages** because it needed no interactive login, and the page was live minutes
  after the links arrived.

## Why it is built this way

**No framework.** A one-page site with three outbound links does not need a build step, a CMS, or a
JavaScript bundle. It is one HTML file and a folder of images, which means it will still work in five
years and can move to any host in a minute.

**Honest ratings.** Two units show 5.0, one shows 4.63. Hiding the lowest number would have been easy
and would have cost the page its credibility with the exact guest who checks Airbnb next.

**It points at Airbnb rather than competing with it.** The page exists so the owner has something to
put on a business card and in an Instagram bio. Bookings still run through the platform that handles
payment, messaging, and cancellation.

## What it demonstrates

The useful skill here is not HTML. It is recognising that a client's "I don't have assets" usually
means the assets exist somewhere already, in a form a script can read, and that the fastest path to
approval is showing someone their own property rather than a template with their name dropped in.
